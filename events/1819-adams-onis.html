TABULA VI · ANNO MDCCCXIX

Adams-Onís Treaty (Florida & Transcontinental Line)

February 22, 1819 Treaty Treaty of Amity, Settlement, and Limits Between the United States of America and His Catholic Majesty

Executive Summary

Spain ceded East and West Florida to the United States and established a definitive transcontinental boundary from the Gulf of Mexico northwest to the 42nd parallel and Pacific Ocean.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

Negotiated by Secretary of State John Quincy Adams and Spanish envoy Luis de Onís following General Andrew Jackson's military incursions during the First Seminole War, the Adams-Onís Treaty (also known as the Transcontinental Treaty) resolved decades of border friction with Spain. Spain formally ceded East Florida and recognized American possession of West Florida. In exchange, the United States assumed up to $5 million in damage claims filed by American citizens against the Spanish government. Crucially, the treaty established a clear stepwise boundary separating the United States from the Viceroyalty of New Spain (Mexico): ascending the Sabine River to 32° N, north to the Red River, up the Red River to 100° W, north to the Arkansas River, following it to its source, north to 42° N, and west along the 42nd parallel to the Pacific Ocean. Spain abandoned all claims north of 42°, granting the U.S. its first internationally recognized treaty claim reaching the Pacific.

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

Florida had served for generations as an autonomous sanctuary for the Seminole Nation and Black Seminoles (formerly enslaved self-emancipated Africans). American planters and the U.S. military targeted Florida precisely to eliminate this refuge and enforce chattel slavery. The transfer initiated decades of violent conflict, known as the Seminole Wars, culminating in the forced removal of thousands of Seminoles to Indian Territory.

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

Based on John Melish's 1816 Map of the United States. Ratified by Spain in 1820 and the U.S. Senate in February 1821; formal transfer of Florida occurred in July 1821.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: FL, AL, MS, LA.
