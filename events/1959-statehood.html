TABULA XIII · ANNO MCMLIX

Alaska & Hawaii Statehood (The 50-State Union)

August 21, 1959 Statehood Alaska Statehood Act (72 Stat. 339) & Hawaii Admission Act (73 Stat. 4)

Executive Summary

Alaska (Jan. 3) and Hawaii (Aug. 21) were admitted to the Union as the 49th and 50th states, establishing the modern geographic framework of the United States.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

After decades as incorporated organized territories governed with limited political representation, Alaska and Hawaii achieved full statehood in 1959. Alaska's admission on January 3, 1959, broke the 47-year hiatus since Arizona's admission in 1912, adding the largest state in land area. Hawaii's admission followed on August 21, 1959, after a landslide territorial plebiscite and congressional compromise pairing a traditionally Democratic territory (Alaska) with a traditionally Republican one (Hawaii, though political allegiances soon shifted). The admission of the two non-contiguous territories completed the modern union of 50 states, permanently represented by the 50-star American flag that debuted on July 4, 1960.

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

Statehood institutionalized state jurisdiction over immense natural resources and lands. In Alaska, disputes over land ownership led directly to the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act of 1971. In Hawaii, the transition entrenched the Department of Hawaiian Home Lands and Office of Hawaiian Affairs, while continuing to spark Native Hawaiian sovereignty and self-determination movements.

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

Formal statutory boundaries established by the Alaska Statehood Act (Public Law 85-508) and Hawaii Admission Act (Public Law 86-3). With these admissions, all 50 modern state jurisdictions were finalized.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: AK, HI.
