TABULA VII · ANNO MDCCCXLV

Annexation of Texas

December 29, 1845 Annexation Joint Resolution of Congress for Annexing Texas to the United States (5 Stat. 797)

Executive Summary

The independent Republic of Texas was admitted directly into the Union as the 28th state, precipitating intense boundary disputes with Mexico over the Rio Grande corridor.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

In 1836, American settlers and Tejanos in the Mexican department of Coahuila y Tejas revolted against the centralist government of Antonio López de Santa Anna, establishing the self-proclaimed Republic of Texas. While Texas sought immediate annexation to the United States, concerns over expanding slavery and war with Mexico delayed admission for nearly a decade. Driven by the expansionist doctrine of 'Manifest Destiny' and President James K. Polk's election, Congress passed a Joint Resolution of Annexation in March 1845. Texas accepted terms and was formally admitted as a state on December 29, 1845. The annexation triggered immediate diplomatic severance by Mexico, which had never recognized Texan independence.

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

The territory claimed by Texas encompassed the sovereign homelands of the powerful Comanche Empire (Comanchería), Kiowa, Apache (Lipan and Mescalero), Tonkawa, and Caddo. The Republic of Texas pursued aggressive, exterminationist policies against Indigenous peoples, driving many tribes from their ancestral hunting grounds through state-sponsored ranger violence.

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

The annexation incorporated a massive boundary dispute. The Republic of Texas claimed all territory south and west to the Rio Grande (including Santa Fe and eastern New Mexico). Mexico maintained that the historic boundary of Texas was the Nueces River, roughly 150 miles to the northeast. President Polk deployed U.S. troops under General Zachary Taylor into the disputed Nueces Strip in early 1846, sparking skirmishes that Polk used to justify a declaration of war against Mexico.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: TX, NM, CO, OK, KS, WY.
