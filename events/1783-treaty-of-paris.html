TABULA II · ANNO MDCCLXXXIII

Treaty of Paris (Original United States Territory)

September 3, 1783 Treaty Definitive Treaty of Peace between the United States and Great Britain

Executive Summary

Great Britain formally acknowledged American independence and ceded sovereign claims over ~890,000 square miles between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mississippi River.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

Signed in Paris by American commissioners John Adams, Benjamin Franklin, and John Jay, alongside David Hartley representing King George III, the Treaty of Paris formally brought the Revolutionary War to an end. Great Britain recognized the United States as free, sovereign, and independent states. The treaty established liberal boundaries for the young nation: extending north to British North America (along the Great Lakes and 45th parallel), west to the Mississippi River, and south to the 31st parallel north (bordering Spanish Florida). This doubled the geographic extent of the new nation beyond the original Appalachian settlements.

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

No Indigenous leaders were present, consulted, or mentioned in the Paris negotiations. Great Britain purported to transfer sovereignty over lands inhabited and defended by sovereign nations including the Shawnee, Miami, Wyandot, Delaware, Ojibwe, Potawatomi, Cherokee, and Creek. Native nations regarded the British cession of their lands as illegitimate, sparking protracted frontier resistance that culminated in the Northwest Indian War (1785–1795).

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

The treaty text relied on John Mitchell's 1755 Map of the British and French Dominions in North America. Mitchell's map contained several geographic errors—most notably mislocating the source of the Mississippi River as lying north of the Lake of the Woods—which necessitated subsequent boundary commissions and treaties well into the 19th century.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: ME, NH, VT, MA, RI, CT, NY, NJ, PA, DE, MD, VA, WV, NC, SC, GA, KY, TN, OH, IN, IL, MI, WI, AL, MS.
