TABULA XI · ANNO MDCCCLXVII

The Alaska Purchase (Cession of Russian America)

March 30, 1867 Purchase Treaty concerning the Cession of the Russian Possessions in North America

Executive Summary

The United States purchased ~586,412 square miles from the Russian Empire for $7.2 million (approx. 2 cents per acre), expanding American territory into the Arctic.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

Following Russia's defeat in the Crimean War and facing mounting logistical difficulties in defending and profiting from its North American colonies (managed by the Russian-American Company), Tsar Alexander II determined to sell Russian America. In late March 1867, Russian Minister Eduard de Stoeckl and U.S. Secretary of State William H. Seward negotiated throughout the night to conclude a treaty of cession for $7.2 million in gold. While political opponents initially derided the purchase as 'Seward's Folly' or 'Seward's Icebox,' the acquisition eliminated imperial Russian presence from the continent, secured strategic maritime access to the North Pacific and Bering Strait, and later yielded immense mineral, oil, timber, and salmon wealth.

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

Russia never exercised effective control over the interior of Alaska; its small trading presence was limited to coastal settlements like Sitka and Kodiak. Alaska was home to deeply established Indigenous cultures: the Tlingit, Haida, and Tsimshian in the southeast; the Yup'ik, Cup'ik, and Inupiat along the western and Arctic coasts; the Unangax̂ (Aleut) across the Aleutian chain; and diverse Athabascan (Dene) peoples in the vast interior. The treaty made no provision for Native land rights, declaring that 'uncivilized native tribes' would merely be subject to U.S. laws. Indigenous land claims remained unrecognized until the Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act (ANCSA) of 1971.

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

The treaty defined the boundary with British Canada along the 141st meridian west and the maritime border through the Bering Strait between Big Diomede (Russian) and Little Diomede (American) islands. Official formal transfer ceremony occurred at Sitka on October 18, 1867.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: AK.
