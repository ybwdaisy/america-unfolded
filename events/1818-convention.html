TABULA V · ANNO MDCCCXVIII

Convention of 1818 (Anglo-American Treaty)

October 20, 1818 Convention Convention of Commerce, Fisheries, and Boundary between His Britannic Majesty and the United States

Executive Summary

Established the 49th parallel north as the boundary from the Lake of the Woods to the Rocky Mountains, ceding the Red River Valley to the U.S. and instituting joint occupation of Oregon.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

Following the War of 1812, diplomats Albert Gallatin and Richard Rush negotiated in London to settle unresolved boundary disputes between the United States and British North America (Canada). The resulting convention established a straight astronomical boundary line along the 49th parallel north (49° N latitude) from the 'Northwesternmost point of the Lake of the Woods' westward to the 'Stony Mountains' (Rocky Mountains). Under this agreement, Britain ceded to the United States the portion of the Red River of the North basin lying south of 49°, while the United States ceded to Britain the northernmost tip of the Louisiana Purchase lying north of 49° (the Milk River and Poplar River drainages). The treaty also agreed to ten years of joint commercial access and occupation in the contested Pacific Northwest (Oregon Country).

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

The 49th parallel was drawn as an abstract mathematical line across thousands of miles of Indigenous homelands, bisecting traditional territories of the Blackfoot Confederacy, Métis Nation, Assiniboine (Nakoda), Plains Cree, and Anishinaabe (Ojibwe). Native peoples dubbed this artificial line the 'Medicine Line' because crossing it caused British or American soldiers and lawmen to abruptly halt their pursuit.

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

Surveyed along the 49th parallel north. Because the 1783 Mitchell map error left an irregular chimney at the Lake of the Woods, the boundary dropped due south from the lake's inlet to intersect the 49th parallel, creating the curious geographic exclave known as the Northwest Angle of Minnesota.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: MN, ND, SD.
