TABULA I · ANNO MDCCLXXVI

Declaration of Independence & The Thirteen Colonies

July 4, 1776 Independence Unanimous Declaration of the Thirteen United States of America

Executive Summary

Delegates of the thirteen North American British colonies declared independence, establishing the United States along the Atlantic seaboard with historic trans-Appalachian claims.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

On July 4, 1776, the Second Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence in Philadelphia, severing political ties with the Kingdom of Great Britain. The new republic comprised thirteen sovereign states: New Hampshire, Massachusetts (including present-day Maine), Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York (claiming present-day Vermont), New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland, Virginia (including present-day West Virginia and Kentucky), North Carolina (including Tennessee), South Carolina, and Georgia. Several states held royal charter claims extending indefinitely westward to the 'South Sea' (Pacific) or the Mississippi River, though British authority, royal proclamations (Proclamation of 1763), and Indigenous nations challenged these inland assertions throughout the Revolutionary War.

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

Long before European colonization, these eastern lands were the sovereign homelands of numerous Indigenous nations, including the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy), Wampanoag, Lenape (Delaware), Powhatan, Cherokee, Muscogee (Creek), Choctaw, and Chickasaw. The Proclamation of 1763 by the British Crown had prohibited colonial settlement west of the Appalachian ridge to preserve peace with Native nations—a constraint that deeply angered colonial land speculators and contributed to the revolutionary movement.

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

Approximate wartime perimeter. Boundaries between colonial charters were frequently overlapping or surveyed with rudimentary compass chains. Actual American military control was concentrated along the Atlantic seaboard and Piedmont, with frontier outposts contesting the interior.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: NH, MA, RI, CT, NY, NJ, PA, DE, MD, VA, NC, SC, GA, ME, VT, WV.
