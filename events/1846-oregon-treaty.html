TABULA VIII · ANNO MDCCCXLVI

The Oregon Treaty

June 15, 1846 Treaty Treaty with Great Britain, in Regard to Limits Westward of the Rocky Mountains

Executive Summary

Extended the 49th parallel international boundary from the Rocky Mountains west to the Pacific Ocean, ending decades of joint British-American occupation.

Historical Narrative & Geopolitical Significance

Since the Convention of 1818, Oregon Country—a vast region spanning from the 42nd parallel (Mexican border) to 54°40' N (Russian America)—had been subject to joint British and American commercial presence, dominated by the Hudson's Bay Company. By the 1840s, thousands of American pioneers traveled the Oregon Trail to settle the fertile Willamette Valley. Expansionist Democrats championed the slogan 'Fifty-Four Forty or Fight!', demanding the entire Pacific Northwest up to Alaska. However, facing imminent war with Mexico, the Polk administration compromised with British Foreign Secretary Lord Aberdeen. The Oregon Treaty of June 1846 extended the existing 49th parallel border to the Strait of Georgia, curving south through the Strait of Juan de Fuca to ensure Britain retained all of Vancouver Island. The treaty added ~286,000 square miles to the United States, providing direct access to deep-water Pacific ports.

Indigenous Sovereignty & Homelands Context

The Pacific Northwest was home to dense, linguistically diverse, and wealthy maritime and riverine Indigenous societies, including the Coast Salish, Nuu-chah-nulth, Chinook, Nez Perce (Nimiipuu), Cayuse, Walla Walla, Yakama, and Umatilla. Within a decade of the treaty, Territorial Governor Isaac Stevens coerced local chiefs into signing the 'Stevens Treaties' (1854–1855), ceding millions of acres while attempting to restrict Native fishing and gathering rights, triggering the Puget Sound War and Yakama War.

Boundary Definition & Cartographic Precision

Extended along the 49th parallel. Ambiguities over the water boundary through Haro Strait versus Rosario Strait left the ownership of the San Juan Islands disputed until 1872, when German Emperor Wilhelm I arbitrated in favor of the United States following the bloodless 'Pig War' standoff.

Modern States Formed or Overlapping

This territorial acquisition includes all or parts of the following modern U.S. states: OR, WA, ID, MT, WY.
