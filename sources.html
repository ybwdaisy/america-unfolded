2. Primary Treaty Texts & Archival Repositories

  • National Archives and Records Administration (NARA): General Records of the United States Government (Record Group 11), Treaties with Great Britain, France, Spain, Mexico, and Russia.
  • The Avalon Project (Yale Law School): Lillian Goldman Law Library, Documents in Law, History and Diplomacy (Treaty of Paris 1783, Louisiana Purchase 1803, Adams-Onís Treaty 1819, Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo 1848).
  • Library of Congress: A Century of Lawmaking for a New Nation: U.S. Congressional Documents and Debates (1774–1875), Statutes at Large, Journals of the Continental Congress.
  • Charles O. Paullin: Atlas of the Historical Geography of the United States (Carnegie Institution of Washington, 1932; edited by John K. Wright).

3. Cartographic Approximation & Precision Disclaimer

Note on Historic Accuracy: While modern GIS coordinate systems utilize the WGS84 datum and precise geodetic calculations, historical treaties from the 18th and 19th centuries defined boundaries using astronomical observations, magnetic compass bearings, watershed divides, and outdated maps (such as John Mitchell's 1755 map or John Melish's 1816 map). Consequently, all historical boundary polygons presented on this platform represent scholarly reconstructions and do not purport to convey contemporary engineering or legal survey precision.
